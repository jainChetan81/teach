teach

A stateful learning workspace. Synced via iCloud, versioned via git, taught in small lessons — built so that finishing is the default, not the exception.

The mission

Own the tools I use every day — from the inside. The concrete goal tying most of these subjects together: carry a live coding-agent session from one device to another — understand how herdr preserves sessions, apply the same model to t3code, and move it over SSH and Tailscale. That single project is the portable-sessions capstone, and it pulls on Rust (herdr's source), TS (t3code), SSH, and VPN internals all at once.

The rule that makes it work: at most two active subjects at any moment. Everything else waits in the backlog without guilt. A subject only becomes active when another one ships its final verification.

What I want to understand

One folder per subject at the root of this workspace — teach/<subject>/ holds that subject's plan, lessons, and reference docs. New subjects get new folders as they arrive.

Motivation design, not motivation hope. Previous plans died of self-propagation failure, so this workspace is built around it: every action item is small enough to finish in one sitting, every subject ends in something you'll use daily, and the checkboxes make progress visible from any device. When you stall, the fix is a smaller next step — never a bigger plan.
