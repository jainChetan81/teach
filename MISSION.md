# Mission: Own the tools I use every day

## Why
Chetan is a strong frontend engineer (React/TS) who uses coding agents, Tauri apps, SSH,
and LLM-powered tools daily — but mostly from the outside. The concrete goal: turn the
tools he already touches into things he understands, can modify, and can extend, so his
workflow compounds instead of just accumulating. The deeper driver he's stated: previous
study plans died from motivation failure, not ability — so the mission is as much
*finishing things* as learning things.

## Success looks like
- Daily-driver modifications shipped: a t3code fork running on his Kimi subscription, and at least one of his own Tauri apps running Rust code he wrote.
- He can whiteboard, from memory: the coding-agent loop, an SSH handshake, and a transformer forward pass.
- kharcha gets an explicit go/no-go decision on the 8-week ML plan — either shipped or deliberately deferred, not drifted into.
- The two-active-tracks rule holds: no more than two tracks active at once; everything else waits in the backlog without guilt.

## Constraints
- Motivation is the scarce resource, not time or intelligence. Every step must be small enough to finish in one sitting; visible progress (checkboxes, meters) is a feature, not decoration.
- Learning must be usable across devices — workspace lives on Desktop (iCloud-synced), progress in browser localStorage, everything git-versioned.
- User prefers HTML for anything he'll read; Markdown is for agent state only.
- Strong prior: React/TS/JS, some ONNX-runtime exposure (loaded a prebuilt model at work), 3–4 self-built Tauri apps in daily use.

## Out of scope (for now)
- New programming languages beyond Rust (no Go/Zig deep dives — read-only where plans require it).
- The JS-runtime-internals and ffmpeg tracks: planned but parked; they activate only when an active track finishes.
- Production-grade ML/MLOps beyond what kharcha needs.
